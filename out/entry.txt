The last of the sun is in the west window. The light lies low across the floorboards in one long amber panel.

A potter's wheel turns in the middle of the room. The clay under her hands is a low, wet hill. The wheel is slowing. Each turn takes longer than the one before.

She sits with her knees apart and her elbows on her thighs. Water runs down her wrist and stops at the crease.

The wheel comes around again. Her thumb lifts on its own and presses a shallow line into the rim. The clay leans into her hand and keeps the shape.

The wheel turns once more and stops. Her hands stay on the clay. The flywheel ticks twice and settles.

Her feet are flat on the floor and her shoulders are low. The light has climbed to the shelf behind her, where six cups stand in a row and hold the last of the sun. The walls keep the heat of the day.

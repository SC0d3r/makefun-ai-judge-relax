The cobbler pulls the door shut behind him and the bell over the door swings on the last of the evening. The swing is wide as a hand, and the note is full. The day is done.

The swing comes down to two fingers, then to a line, and the note drains on the same steps, with less ring each time. The cat's chin comes down onto its paws a bit at a time and rests, and does not lift again.

The note goes.

Then nothing.

A mended shoe sits done on the counter. The wax is warm where his thumb pressed it. The clock over the door goes on past the hour, on and on.

Tick by tick.

The last light goes.

The street lets go of its light, and the dark takes the glass of the door. The shine on the wax holds and holds. Nothing is left to do for it to keep.

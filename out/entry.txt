At ten past six the cobbler pulls the shop door shut behind him, and the bell over the latch keeps swinging.

The swing narrows to the width of a hand, and its ring is full. To the width of two fingers, and the ring has less in it. To a line, and the sound has no ring left in it. To nothing, and it does not move.

A cat sleeps on the counter. Its chin lowers onto its paws a little at a time, and rests there, and does not lift again.

A broom leans under the bench where he left it, and the dust around it keeps its shape.

The mended shoe sits on the rack, wax warm where his thumb pressed it, holding a low shine.

The clock over the door goes on past the hour, tick by tick. The colour of wet slate leaves the street, and the dark takes the glass.

The shine on the wax will keep until morning, and nothing needs to be done for it to keep.

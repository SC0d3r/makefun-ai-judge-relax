The drops land on the canvas a hand apart, each drop its own drum, and you are in the bag, warm, with the zip someone shut from outside.

A coat that is not yours lies over the bag, left there, and the rain slows. The flask sits by your knee, still warm where a hand set it down.

The drops come two fingers apart now, and the drum thins into a tap. By the bag, the torch lays a hand of light on the canvas.
A line apart.

Then the tap is a tick, and the ticks come slower. The hand of light on the canvas thins to two fingers of light. Your own hand opens, and it stays open. No one asks.

Two fingers thin to a line, rain and light on the same slow beat. The moon moves off the canvas, and the hour goes by with no clock.
The light goes.

One last drop lands, and no drop follows. The tick is gone. The canvas takes the dark on, all of it.

The dark is on your side of the canvas, and no one needs you before morning.
